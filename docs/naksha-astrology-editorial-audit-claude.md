## Executive Judgment

Naksha has a complete lookup system and an incomplete interpretation system.

Every supported combination returns text. The text is grammatical, gentle, and almost never fatalistic. In most families, though, it describes the ingredients of a configuration rather than the configuration. Planet-in-sign entries for the Sun and Moon come closest to real interpretation. Planet-in-house and house-sign entries are template output: the same opening, the same hedges, and a closing "Growth comes through…" sentence in 254 of 264 entries. Natal aspects are not interpreted at all. Forty-five planet pairs share five glossary sentences, several of which are wrong for the pairs they label. Daily and weekly guidance concatenate abstract fragments, rank transits by orb alone, and can switch today's headline twelve times in one day.

The best writing in the product is the Sky Now pair content. It is concrete, planet-specific, observable, and correctly framed for a shared sky. Its aspect layer, however, gives conjunction, trine, and sextile one "potential" sentence, and square and opposition one "friction" sentence, for 44 of 45 pairs.

Measured against the product's own test, "After reading this, does a normal person understand what this configuration might actually mean in lived experience?":

| Surface | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Planet-in-sign, Sun and Moon | Often yes; needs polish |
| Planet-in-sign, Mercury through Saturn | Sometimes; often a sign description carrying the planet's name |
| Planet-in-sign, Uranus through Pluto | No; generational placements are written as personal identity |
| Planet-in-house | Rarely; keyword substitution plus a self-improvement clause |
| Houses | As definitions, yes; as interpretation, no |
| House-sign | Rarely; three sign adjectives applied to a house domain |
| Natal aspects | No |
| Sky Now | Yes for the planet pair; no for the specific aspect |
| Today's Energy | Rarely; stitched fragments with weak transit-to-practice links |
| Weekly Forecast | No; a list of transits, not a week |
| Reflection prompts | Good questions, weakly connected to the astrology |
| Practices | Generic wellness and productivity, loosely matched |

The system is safe to read and not yet worth reading closely. It should not enter release-candidate freeze before the P0 items are resolved. The natal aspect, Sky Now aspect, daily, and weekly layers need architectural redesign, not polish.

## What Naksha Does Well

- **Complete, deterministic coverage.** 120 planet-in-sign, 144 house-sign, 120 planet-in-house, and 45 Sky Now pair entries exist with no gaps. Every guidance record has a stable id, and prompts and practices carry source ids, so a rewrite can be traced.
- **Restraint about prediction.** The corpus almost never forecasts events or uses fate language. Sky Now closes with the right disclaimer: "A symbolic reading of the shared sky. Take what resonates with your experience." Slow pairs carry a background note. Reflection surfaces say "Use this as reflection, not a diagnosis."
- **Sky Now pair writing.** Friction lines such as Moon–Pluto's "A strong reaction may carry more history than the present situation alone can explain" and Mercury–Jupiter's "A compelling story may move faster than the evidence supporting it" describe something a person can notice. They pass the label-removal test.
- **Light and shadow in planet-in-sign.** Nearly every planet-in-sign entry carries a constructive and a difficult expression, and the difficult side is stated without contempt.
- **No dignity moralizing.** Moon in Scorpio, Mars in Cancer, Venus in Scorpio, and Mercury in Pisces are not called weak or fallen.
- **Perceptive Moon writing.** Moon in Sagittarius ("you instinctively zoom out when emotions get heavy, searching for the lesson or bigger meaning") and Moon in Capricorn ("Achievement and productivity can become emotional armor") are specific and recognizable.
- **Well-made reflection questions.** "Are you waiting for useful information, or for a level of certainty this choice cannot provide?" is psychologically useful and avoids platitude.
- **Sharp guidance "watch for" lines.** The natal-target Saturn line, "Fear of falling short may turn preparation into delay or standards into punishment," and the Neptune line, "Hope or projection may supply missing details and make a preference feel like a fact," are excellent raw material.

## What Naksha Fundamentally Lacks

1. **Synthesis.** Every combined reading is a concatenation. Planet-in-sign and planet-in-house are glued with "This tends to show up most clearly when." Transit, natal target, and aspect are stacked as three unrelated sentences. Sky Now appends a generic aspect sentence to a pair theme. Nothing interprets the interaction.
2. **Natal aspect interpretation.** The most psychologically specific layer of a natal chart has five sentences for 225 combinations.
3. **Aspect differentiation.** Where pair content exists, in Sky Now, the aspect only switches between two texts.
4. **Lived-experience translation.** Entries name traits and themes such as depth, transformation, and authenticity. They rarely say what a person might feel, do, avoid, repeat, or notice.
5. **Hierarchy.** Nothing in a chart is weighted. There is no chart ruler, no stellium recognition, no angularity, no Sun–Moon–rising triad, and no aspect pattern. Daily guidance ranks transits by orb alone.
6. **Context tiers.** Personal placements, generational placements, rising-sign-determined house signs, and the shared sky are all written in the same intimate second person.
7. **Time awareness.** There are no retrogrades, stations, lunar phases, ingresses, or transit durations. The weekly forecast cannot tell a stationary Venus from a week-long theme.
8. **A written editorial standard.** The corpus reads as the output of several templates, not of one editor working to one rubric.
9. **Whole Sign technique.** The app uses Whole Sign houses but not the interpretive practice that gives them meaning. Empty houses are never read through their ruling planet.

## Astrology Accuracy Findings

**A1. Natal aspect texts misdescribe many of the pairs they label (P0).** Every natal pair shows the short text for its aspect type:

| Aspect | Text shown for every planet pair |
|---|---|
| Conjunction | "Two energies fused together, amplifying each other." |
| Opposition | "A polarity that asks for balance and integration." |
| Square | "Friction that pushes you toward growth and action." |
| Trine | "A natural flow of energy and ease between planets." |
| Sextile | "Light opportunities that respond well to gentle effort." |

These are not just generic; they are wrong for many common pairs. Saturn conjunct Neptune is not two energies amplifying each other: one gives form and the other dissolves it, and the conjunction is classically a tension. Moon square Neptune and Mercury square Neptune do not push anyone toward action; their signature is blur, uncertainty, and porous perception. Moon conjunct Saturn constrains the Moon rather than amplifying it. "A natural flow of energy and ease" hides the usual difficulty of Moon trine Saturn: emotional reserve that passes for maturity. The unrendered long text goes further and calls trines "things that just work."

**A2. Generational placements are written as individual identity (P0).** None of the 36 Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto sign entries says the placement is shared by everyone born across a span of years. Six contain "you're here to," three use "wired," and the shorts make strong identity claims:

- Pluto in Scorpio, everyone born roughly 1983–1995: "You are wired for profound emotional and psychological transformation."
- Uranus in Aquarius, roughly 1995–2003: "You’re naturally ahead of your time and wired for innovation."
- Uranus in Leo, roughly 1955–1962: "Your self-expression is unique, bold, and impossible to ignore."
- Neptune in Capricorn, roughly 1984–1998: all three sample charts receive "You seek to merge dreams with responsibility and achievement" as a personal summary.

Saturn's two-and-a-half-year sign cohorts get childhood claims, for example "You may have felt blocked or hesitant about taking initiative early in life" for Saturn in Aries. Several outer-planet entries cannot occur in a living person's natal chart (Pluto in Aries, Taurus, or Gemini; Neptune in Taurus or Gemini), yet they describe personal experiences such as "You may experience loss or upheaval." That shows the family was filled by template rather than written for use.

**A3. Whole Sign technical errors (P0).** The house meanings label the 10th house "(MC)" and the 4th "(IC)." In Whole Sign houses the Midheaven degree can fall in the 9th, 10th, or 11th whole-sign house, and at high latitudes in the 8th or 12th. The app never computes the MC, yet tells every user that their 10th house is the MC. I computed the MC for six sample charts. In Chart A it is 5° Sagittarius, in the 11th whole-sign house. In a Stockholm chart for 10 March 1992, 02:00 UTC, it falls in the 12th. The Ascendant and Descendant labels are correct.

**A4. Sign, house, and planet conflation (P2).** Seventeen entries state "this is a natural placement, as X rules this house." Nine are house-sign entries (Cancer 4th, Leo 5th, Virgo 6th, Libra 7th, Scorpio 8th, Sagittarius 9th, Capricorn 10th, Aquarius 11th, Pisces 12th). Eight are planet-in-house entries (Moon 4th, Mercury 3rd, Venus 7th, Jupiter 9th, Saturn 10th, Uranus 11th, Neptune 12th, Pluto 8th). This is the modern "twelve-letter alphabet" convention presented as fact. It is applied inconsistently: Aries 1st, Taurus 2nd, Gemini 3rd, Sun 5th, Mars 1st, Venus 2nd, and Mercury 6th get no such note. It also sits awkwardly in a Whole Sign system, whose Hellenistic source tradition puts the planetary joys elsewhere: Mercury in the 1st, the Moon in the 3rd, Saturn in the 12th.

**A5. Planet and sign meanings collapse into each other (P2).** Sun in Aries, Mars in Aries, and Aries on the 1st house describe nearly the same person and prescribe the same remedy, patience and pacing. Neptune in Pisces, Moon in Pisces, Neptune in the 12th, and Pisces on the 12th are close to interchangeable. The planet's function rarely changes what the sign is said to do.

**A6. Aspect type overrides the planets in daily guidance (P0).** Tone is assigned by aspect type alone: every square is "challenging," every trine "supportive," every conjunction "intensifying" and "high." Venus square Jupiter is therefore "challenging," and the generic square warning tells the user to "Watch for repeating the same forceful response when the underlying conflict requires a different method." Venus–Jupiter squares are classically about excess and overindulgence, not force. Chart B (Venus square natal Uranus) and Chart C (Venus square natal Jupiter) both receive that "forceful response" warning on 2 October.

**A7. Conjunctions are read as soft in Sky Now (P1).** For 44 pairs, a conjunction gets the pair's "potential" sentence. Mars conjunct Neptune reads "A creative or compassionate purpose may give effort a direction that feels worthwhile," omitting the classic low-energy, foggy, or misdirected side. Mars–Saturn, Mars–Pluto, and Saturn–Pluto conjunctions get one-sided readings in the same way.

**A8. Dignity mentions are selective (P2).** "At home" is stated for Mercury in Gemini, Venus in Taurus, Jupiter in Sagittarius, Saturn in Capricorn, and the modern outer-planet rulers. It is omitted for Mars in Aries, Venus in Libra, Mercury in Virgo, Mars in Scorpio, Jupiter in Pisces, and Saturn in Aquarius. Saturn's traditional domicile in Aquarius matters for the Saturn in Aquarius deep review. Avoiding debility labels is defensible; mentioning domicile selectively implies a lesser status for the omitted placements.

**A9. Time-based products ignore standard astrological events (P1).** Venus stations retrograde at about 8°28′ Scorpio on 3 October 2026. The codebase contains no retrograde or station logic, so the weekly forecast repeats the same Venus aspect row on five or six of seven days. Sky Now's Sun–Moon aspects are the lunar phases, yet "New Moon" and "Full Moon" are never named, and the Full Moon and both quarter Moons share one "friction" text.

**A10. Configurations are invisible (P2).** Sky Now on 2 October 2026 contains a T-square: Mars 2°36′ Leo opposite Pluto 3°06′ Aquarius, both squared by Mercury 2°48′ Scorpio, all within half a degree. The user sees three unrelated cards.

**A11. House meanings import sign and planet associations (P3).** The 11th house "governs … technological spaces," an Aquarius and Uranus association, and the 12th "includes karmic patterns."

**A12. Daily significance is astrologically naive (P1).** The daily headline is the smallest orb among transiting Moon, Sun, Mercury, Venus, and Mars to natal planets at the instant the dashboard loads. A Moon aspect lasting a few hours outranks Mars square the natal Sun. Saturn and Jupiter appear only in the weekly forecast, and Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto transits appear nowhere.

## Human-Legibility Findings

The brief's central failure, explaining astrology instead of interpreting it, is widespread in these families:

| Family | How it explains instead of interpreting | Prevalence |
|---|---|---|
| Natal aspects | Defines the aspect type | Every pair |
| Guidance aspect dynamics | "Two functions converge, increasing concentration without deciding how that concentration will be used." | 5 of 5 |
| Daily transit summary | "This brings [keyword list] into contact with [keyword list]." | Every day with a transit |
| Weekly themes | "It connects [keyword list] with [keyword list]." | Every theme |
| House meanings | Textbook definition of the house | 12 of 12 |
| House-sign | Sign adjectives applied to the house's nouns | Nearly all 144 |
| Sky Now "dynamic" line | Defines the aspect type | 225 of 225 |

Abstract vocabulary crowds out lived detail. I counted abstract terms (transform-, energy, depth, intens-, authentic-, meaningful, profound-, growth, empower-, journey, soul, light) against concrete life nouns (money, job, partner, friend, sleep, meal, deadline, argument, and similar):

| Family | Abstract terms per 1,000 words | Concrete life nouns per 1,000 words |
|---|---|---|
| Planet-in-house | 33.1 | 7.6 |
| House-sign | 25.5 | 10.2 |
| Planet-in-sign | 17.0 | 4.7 |
| Sky Now pairs | 4.3 | 6.6 |

Sky Now is the only family where concrete language outweighs abstraction, and it is also the most readable.

Specific failures:

- **"Transformation" stands in for interpretation.** Pluto in the 8th uses "transformation" three times, alongside "profound," "intense," "deep," "rebirth," and "change," and never says what changes or how it feels.
- **Unexplained life areas.** Daily "Life area: House 10 · vocation, visibility, responsibility, and long-term direction" never says why this house is relevant (the transiting planet is passing through it) or what to look for there.
- **Exposed computation.** "within 0.01°," "This pattern appears in 7 of 7 sampled days," "Active on 6 of 7 sampled days."
- **Undefined terms.** "Natal Jupiter," "conjoins," and "forms a sextile to" appear with no explanation for a reader who does not know astrology.
- **Planning jargon in prose.** "Two functions," "concentration," and "the blocked function" come from the system design, not from the reader's world.

**Label-removal test.** I stripped labels from a sample of entries and tried to recover the configuration. Sky Now friction lines identified the pair in most cases. Sun and Moon sign entries identified the sign and usually the planet. Planet-in-house and house-sign entries identified the house domain but rarely the planet or sign, because the planet- or sign-specific content is a three-adjective slot. Natal aspects, guidance aspect dynamics, prompts, and practices could not be identified at all.

## Planet-in-Sign Findings

This is the strongest natal family, and it is uneven by planet.

| Planet group | Synthesis of planet function and sign style | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sun | Fair to good | Describes identity in action; overlaps heavily with Mars and the rising sign |
| Moon | Good | The best entries in the natal corpus; childhood claims in Capricorn |
| Mercury | Fair | Some vivid entries (Cancer, Pisces); many shorts are sign adjectives |
| Venus | Weak | All 12 entries are about romance; money, taste, pleasure, and receiving are missing |
| Mars | Fair | Consistent drive-plus-anger structure; the anger line is formulaic |
| Jupiter | Weak | Sign description plus "growth"; 10 of 12 use "You believe…" |
| Saturn | Weak to fair | Sign description plus early-life difficulty |
| Uranus, Neptune, Pluto | Misframed | Generational placements written as personal identity and mission |

**Venus is reduced to romance.** The section's own header promises "love style, attraction, values, pleasure." Money appears in none of the 12 Venus entries. Beauty or aesthetics appear only in Taurus, Libra, and Pisces. A reader asking what they value, enjoy, and find beautiful gets a dating profile.

**Sign stereotypes recur across planets.** Eight of ten Aquarius entries invoke detachment or aloofness. All ten Pisces entries prescribe grounding or boundaries. Six of ten Capricorn entries prescribe softening, rest, warmth, or emotional release. Each sign has one shadow, and every planet in that sign inherits it.

**Slow planets are written too personally.** No entry acknowledges a cohort. Saturn signs, shared by everyone born in a two-and-a-half-year window, read as personal lessons with childhood framing. Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto signs, shared across 7 to 30 years of births, read as identity and destiny.

**Structure.** 117 of 120 longs open with "With your X in Y," "With X in Y," or "X in Y," and the challenge sentence sits in the second paragraph almost everywhere. The Sun and Moon longs are hard-wrapped, which breaks lines mid-sentence on screen (P3-1).

**The composed summary contradicts itself.** The placement list and each planet page lead with the sign short and the house short joined by "This tends to show up most clearly when." The result is often incoherent:

- Chart C, Mercury in Sagittarius in the 6th: "Your thinking is expansive, philosophical, and future-oriented. This tends to show up most clearly when your thinking is analytical, precise, and focused on solving problems and improving systems."
- Chart A, Moon in Virgo in the 8th: "You find emotional security through order, usefulness, and clear understanding. This tends to show up most clearly when your emotions are intense, deep, and connected to transformation, intimacy, and vulnerability."
- Chart A, Mercury in Capricorn in the 12th: "Your mind is structured, strategic, and goal-oriented. This tends to show up most clearly when your thinking is introspective, intuitive, and connected to the subconscious or unseen."

The glue asserts a conditional relationship that the two halves cannot support, in the first text a user reads about each planet.

### Deep review: Sun in Aries

> "With your Sun in Aries, you’re built to initiate. … You’re straightforward, impatient with stagnation, and often the first to speak or act. … so that your fire energizes your life and relationships rather than burning you out or burning bridges."

- **What works:** An active, confident voice. "Often the first to speak or act" is observable. "Burning you out or burning bridges" names an inner cost and a relational cost in one image.
- **What is missing:** What the Sun does in a chart: the organizing sense of purpose, what makes a person feel alive and like themselves. The inner experience: restlessness when blocked, needing a challenge to feel real, vulnerability under directness, being experienced by others as too much. Any distinction from Mars in Aries or Aries rising, which say nearly the same thing.
- **What feels generic:** "Carving your own path rather than waiting for permission," "impatient with stagnation," and the patience prescription shared by every Aries entry.
- **What a human may still not understand:** What "core self" means, and how this differs from simply being confident.
- **What the interpretation should accomplish:** Show identity becoming real through initiating; vitality tied to challenge and beginnings; the risk that identity depends on winning or being first; and maturity as courage that can tolerate waiting and follow-through.

### Deep review: Moon in Scorpio

> "Moon in Scorpio feels everything strongly, even if little is shown outwardly. You may be private, protective, or suspicious when it comes to vulnerability. Emotional trust is earned, not given quickly. Transformative healing is possible when you allow yourself to be seen by people who prove safe, letting connection soften the need for control."

- **What works:** "Feels everything strongly, even if little is shown outwardly" is the right core. There is no "fall" moralizing. "Trust is earned" is plainly useful.
- **What is missing:** The Moon as a safety mechanism: what makes this person feel safe (loyalty, emotional honesty, privacy, knowing where they stand). What the vigilance feels like from inside: testing people, reading undercurrents, remembering hurts, all-or-nothing attachment. Its gifts: steadiness in a crisis, willingness to stay with someone else's pain, refusal of emotional pretense. Jealousy and possessiveness, clichés but real themes, are left out rather than handled well.
- **What feels generic:** "Transformative healing is possible" is therapeutic filler. The voice shifts from "Moon in Scorpio feels" to "You may be."
- **What a human may still not understand:** What "depth" or "the need for control" look like in an ordinary relationship or week.
- **What the interpretation should accomplish:** Explain, in human terms, the tension between the Moon's need for comfort and a sign that prefers truth to comfort, with one constructive and one difficult pattern a person could recognize.

### Deep review: Mercury in Pisces

> "Mercury in Pisces thinks in images, feelings, and impressions rather than strict logic. You absorb information subtly, often understanding things before you can articulate them. … The challenge is clarity. … When balanced, your mind becomes a bridge between imagination and meaning, logic and empathy."

- **What works:** "Understanding things before you can articulate them" is one of the best lines in the natal corpus. It describes a real experience.
- **What is missing:** Concrete difficulty: losing track of details, being swayed by the last person who spoke, telling people what they want to hear, trouble with linear explanation, being misread as vague. Concrete gifts: empathic listening, metaphor, reading tone, seeing connections across contexts.
- **What feels generic:** "Grounding your insights in structure helps prevent confusion or overwhelm" and "a bridge between imagination and meaning, logic and empathy." The ending could close any Pisces or Neptune entry.
- **What a human may still not understand:** What this means at school, at work, in an argument, or when giving directions.
- **What the interpretation should accomplish:** Convey the experience of intuitive thinking in a linear world, and the practical habits that make it reliable.

### Deep review: Venus in Capricorn

> "With Venus in Capricorn, you approach relationships thoughtfully and with intention. … Love is something you build, not rush. You may guard your heart carefully, opening slowly over time. Allowing warmth and vulnerability alongside responsibility helps your love feel emotionally fulfilling, not just secure."

- **What works:** "Love is something you build, not rush" is concise and accurate. The entry avoids the status-seeking cliché.
- **What is missing:** Venus beyond romance: Capricorn Venus's values and tastes (quality, restraint, the classic, pleasure that has been earned); how it shows care (reliability, providing, showing up); how it receives care, often poorly; and its difficult forms (love as a performance of responsibility, testing a partner's seriousness, status-consciousness in attraction, withholding affection until it feels safe).
- **What feels generic:** The ending, the standard Capricorn corrective.
- **What a human may still not understand:** How this shows up with money, friends, gifts, and enjoyment.
- **What the interpretation should accomplish:** Describe a value system and an affection style, including how this person receives care.

### Deep review: Mars in Cancer

> "With Mars in Cancer, your actions are influenced by feelings and emotional safety. You’re motivated to protect yourself and those you care about, often acting behind the scenes rather than directly. Anger can be indirect or suppressed, surfacing later as moodiness. Developing clear boundaries and direct expression allows your drive to feel empowered instead of defensive."

- **What works:** No "weak Mars" framing. The protective motive and indirect anger are central to the placement.
- **What is missing:** The ferocity that appears when someone they love is threatened. Drive that rises and falls with emotional security. What indirect anger looks like: sulking, withdrawal, guilt, sideways remarks. The fear underneath: that asserting a want will cost closeness.
- **What feels generic:** "Developing clear boundaries and direct expression allows your drive to feel empowered instead of defensive" could end most Mars entries.
- **What a human may still not understand:** Why they can fight hard for others but struggle to ask for what they want themselves.
- **What the interpretation should accomplish:** Show desire and anger operating through the need for safety and belonging, with one recognizable protective strength and one recognizable avoidance pattern.

### Deep review: Saturn in Aquarius

> "With Saturn in Aquarius, lessons revolve around belonging and authenticity. You may feel like an outsider or struggle to balance independence with community. Maturity brings the ability to contribute uniquely while remaining connected, turning innovation into meaningful impact."

- **What works:** Belonging versus individuality is a defensible theme.
- **What is missing:** The cohort: everyone born roughly 1991–1994 and 2020–2023. What Saturn does in a chart: where a person feels tested, where competence comes slowly, where fear and rules live. Saturn's traditional domicile here, acknowledged for Capricorn but not for Aquarius. The specific Aquarian tests: the group's rules against personal conscience, rigid ideals, intellectualizing as a defense, responsibility toward a community.
- **What feels generic:** "Turning innovation into meaningful impact" could close any Aquarius entry.
- **What a human may still not understand:** What "collective responsibility" means in their life, and that the placement is shared with their whole school cohort.
- **What the interpretation should accomplish:** Name the cohort, describe the Saturnian pressure in Aquarian terms, and send the reader to Saturn's house for the personal expression.

### Deep review: Neptune in Pisces

> "Neptune is at home in Pisces, amplifying empathy, creativity, and spiritual awareness. You may feel connected to unseen realms, emotions, or collective suffering. Learning boundaries is essential. When grounded, your sensitivity becomes a powerful channel for healing, art, and compassion."

- **What works:** The symbolic associations are correct.
- **What is missing:** That the placement is shared by everyone born roughly 2011–2026, so its natal readers are children. Guest charts make this reachable today: a parent opening a child's chart reads "You may feel connected to unseen realms, emotions, or collective suffering," addressed to the parent. There is no pointer to Neptune's house or aspects, which carry the personal expression.
- **What feels generic:** The whole entry could describe the Moon in Pisces, the Sun in Pisces, or Neptune in the 12th.
- **What a human may still not understand:** Why their placement is the same as their classmates'.
- **What the interpretation should accomplish:** Describe a generational atmosphere (collective imagination, porous boundaries, immersion, compassion and escapism) framed as shared, and hand personal meaning to the house and aspects.

## Planet-in-House Findings

**Template dominance.** All 120 longs open "With the X in the Nth House," and the final sentence of all 120 begins "Growth comes through." 118 contain "You may," 99 contain a "so [result]" clause, and 65 a "rather than" contrast. Shorts are slot-filled by planet: all 12 Sun shorts begin "Your identity," all 12 Moon shorts "Your emotions," and all 12 Mercury shorts "Your thinking."

**Keyword substitution rather than synthesis.** Entries pair planet keywords with house keywords, then attach the same advice regardless of planet. The 3rd house shows it clearly:

- Sun 3rd: "…so your intelligence becomes integrated wisdom rather than constant movement alone."
- Mercury 3rd: "…so your intelligence becomes structured knowledge rather than scattered information."
- Jupiter 3rd: "…so your intellectual expansion becomes integrated wisdom rather than scattered information."

All ten 12th-house entries resolve to awareness, grounding, or clarity ("so what is hidden becomes a source of mastery rather than limitation," "so your inner world becomes a source of healing rather than confusion"). The planet changes; the advice does not.

**Missing lived detail.** The family has the highest abstraction density in the corpus, 33 abstract terms per 1,000 words. Few entries say what someone with the placement might do on an ordinary day. Difficult expression is usually one hedged clause, often "However, X may arise" or "However, X can arise."

**Outcome claims despite heavy hedging.** "You may experience sudden changes in finances" (Uranus 2nd), "cycles of gain and loss" (Pluto 2nd), "Financial situations may fluctuate" (Neptune 2nd), "There is often a natural ability to attract partners" (Venus 7th), "they often carry depth and longevity" (Saturn 7th), "reshaping your identity multiple times" (Pluto 1st).

**Outer-planet houses are where personalization belongs.** Pluto's sign is generational; Pluto's house depends on birth time and place. The family writes outer-planet houses with the same template and never makes the distinction.

### Deep review: Sun in the 1st House

- **What works:** "Others may notice you quickly" is observable.

## House Findings

- **Register.** The 12 house meanings are textbook definitions. That suits a "what is this house?" block, but on house pages the definition is followed immediately by the house-sign text, so users read two definitions in a row and no interpretation.
- **Accuracy.** The "(MC)" and "(IC)" labels are wrong in Whole Sign (A3). "Technological spaces" (11th) and "karmic patterns" (12th) import planet and sign associations.
- **Softened significations.** Death and endings never appear for the 8th, only "deep metamorphosis." Illness never appears for the 6th, nor confinement or institutions for the 12th. Modern softening is defensible, but euphemism leaves users who have read elsewhere unsure what the app means.
- **Inconsistency.** Only the 1st house says what planets there do: "Planets here strongly shape your instinctive behavior and life direction."
- **Empty houses.** Nothing tells a user what an empty house means, though most houses in most charts are empty.

## House-Sign Findings

**Template dominance.** All 144 longs open "With X on the Nth House," and the final sentence of 134 begins "Growth comes through." In houses 2, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, and 11, all twelve shorts share one frame with three sign adjectives slotted in:

- "You pursue your career and public path with discipline, responsibility, and a strong drive for achievement." (Capricorn 10th)
- "You pursue your career and public path through communication, adaptability, and diverse skills." (Gemini 10th)

**Sign-corrective cliché.** The growth clause is a function of the sign, not the house:

| Sign | Typical growth clause | Entries |
|---|---|---|
| Aries | "developing patience" | 4 of 12, plus "consistency" and "pacing" variants |
| Libra | "maintaining your [direction, individuality, voice]" | 5 of 12 |
| Sagittarius | "grounding your [vision, insights]" | 5 of 12 |
| Pisces | "grounding" | 9 of 12 |
| Capricorn | "allowing [vulnerability, flexibility, warmth]" | 6 of 12 |
| Aquarius | "integrating emotional presence" | 5 of 12 |

Across house-sign and planet-in-house, "grounding your" heads 33 growth clauses.

**The sign on an empty house is overstated.** In Whole Sign houses the twelve house signs are fixed by the rising sign. Every Aries-rising user receives the same twelve house-sign paragraphs, so the layer is effectively twelve rising-sign profiles. The content still writes each paragraph as a personal verdict ("You pursue money, resources, and self-worth intensely, strategically…"). In Whole Sign practice the decisive information for a house is the condition and placement of its ruling planet, which the content never uses.

**Contradictions are left unreconciled.** Chart C, with Cancer rising, is told its 7th house means "You seek relationships that are stable, committed, and built on responsibility and long-term goals." On the planet tab it is told Neptune in its 7th makes relationships "idealistic, intuitive, and may involve projection or blurred boundaries." Both are written as the whole truth, and nothing reconciles them.

**Childhood claims.** Four 4th-house entries assign an early environment from the sign alone: Aries ("required you to be strong, assertive, or self-reliant early on"), Leo ("strong personalities or a desire for recognition within your early environment"), Scorpio ("your early environment and emotional roots may have been intense, private, or transformative"), and Capricorn ("responsibility, pressure").

**Unusually strong:** the 2nd house set is the most concrete: Gemini's "more than one income stream," Scorpio's "ability to rebuild after loss," Virgo's "worry … around not doing enough." **Unusually weak:** the 12th house set, where every entry is a variation of "your subconscious may…"; the 8th house set of adjectives about an "approach to intimacy"; and the 9th and 11th sets, which are nearly interchangeable between signs.

**What natal aspects must explain and do not:** the internal contradiction or alliance between the two functions; how it shows in behavior; how it shows in relationships where relevant; the constructive integration; the difficult expression; and how the pattern tends to mature. ## Sky Now Findings

**The framing is right.** The card says "The planets in the zodiac right now." Each reading closes with "A symbolic reading of the shared sky. Take what resonates with your experience." Tapping a planet shows only its position, so no natal text is misapplied to the shared sky. Slow pairs carry a background note.

**The pair layer is strong.** The 45 titles are well chosen: "Desire and reciprocity," "Care that can last," "Imagination and discernment." The friction sentences are the best writing in the product, the practices are concrete and proportionate, and the reflections are pair-specific.

**The aspect layer collapses. This is a major finding.** `getSkyAspectMeaning` returns the pair's "potential" sentence for conjunction, trine, and sextile, and its "friction" sentence for square and opposition. The practice and reflection never change; only the generic "dynamic" sentence differs. For 44 of 45 pairs:

- conjunction, trine, and sextile share one meaning, one practice, and one reflection;
- square and opposition share one meaning, one practice, and one reflection.

Moon–Saturn alone has five meanings and five practices, and its reflection is still shared.

**Consequences.**

- Conjunctions of difficult pairs read as opportunities (A7).
- Oppositions lose their relational, projective, see-saw quality, and squares lose their internal, action-forcing quality.
- Trines lose their risk of complacency, and sextiles lose the sense of an opening that needs initiative.
- The test that requires five distinct readings per pair passes only because the dynamic sentence differs, so it certifies a distinction that the reading does not make.

**There is no collective layer.** Sky Now describes a shared sky but speaks only to an individual. Uranus trine Pluto, Neptune sextile Pluto, and Uranus sextile Neptune are among the slowest and most collective signatures in today's sky. Their readings are personal tips with a disclaimer appended. Neptune and Pluto have been in or near sextile since the mid-twentieth century, yet the pairing is presented like any other current aspect.

**Lunar phases go unnamed.** Sun–Moon aspects are the most widely understood astrological rhythm, and Sky Now never says "New Moon" or "Full Moon."

**Configurations go unrecognized.** See A10.

**Second-person drift.** Most meanings are phrased as possibilities, but some address the reader's worth: "Responsibility may feel like a measure of your worth, especially when progress is slow" (Sun–Saturn friction). That is acceptable under "take what resonates," but the boundary should be a deliberate choice.

**Today's sky, 2 October 2026, 15:00 UTC.** Eleven aspects display. Individually, the fast-planet readings are good: Mercury square Mars ("Speed or certainty may make it tempting to answer before understanding the question") and Mercury square Pluto ("a suspicion that every word hides something") are perceptive. Together they describe one T-square that the card cannot name. Mars opposite Pluto at 0.48°, the base of that T-square, gets the same text it would get as a square. Three of the eleven are slow aspects, each with personal-practice advice and the same appended note.

## Practice / Reflection Findings

**Prompts (34).** They are grammatically varied, non-judgmental, and often perceptive. Their weakness is linkage, not quality.

- Most are planet-agnostic decision or communication hygiene, for example "What do you know, what are you assuming, and what still needs to be asked?"
- Several are exactly the weak genericity the brief describes: "What would make your inner or physical space feel a little more supportive today?", "What can be paused or set down long enough for you to hear what you need?", "Which future goal could benefit from collaboration or a shared perspective?", and "Where are your time, attention, or resources going, and does that reflect your values?"
- Selection is by source-id or tag overlap. A prompt written for Mercury–Neptune ("Leave room for the unknown") is chosen for Mercury trine Jupiter because both involve Mercury.

**Practices (24).** Mostly productivity and wellness techniques: "Single-task reset," "Completion pass," "Tidy one small area," "Quiet the inputs," "Short walk and reset," "Five-sense grounding," "Specific gratitude list." Each is reasonable on its own. Almost none refers to a planet or mechanism, the pick is a hash among loose matches, and all of them are labeled "Grounding practice."

(Moon–Pluto), "What do I know, what do I sense, and what still needs checking?" (Mercury–Neptune), "Where would pacing serve me better than pushing harder?" **Mismatches in generated output.** A gratitude list for Venus square natal Uranus; a gratitude list for Mercury trine natal Jupiter; "Completion pass" for Jupiter trine natal Saturn; "Question the delay" for Moon opposite natal Mercury.

## Voice Findings

**The current voice** is earnest modern self-help astrology: second person, heavily hedged, triads of adjectives, abstract nouns, and a therapeutic resolution at the end of every entry.

**It is not one voice.** Four registers coexist:

| Register | Where | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Intimate and observant | Moon signs, Sky Now pairs | "you instinctively zoom out when emotions get heavy" |
| Template self-help | Planet-in-house, house-sign, Jupiter and Saturn signs | "Growth comes through grounding your vision, so…" |
| Clinical and abstract | Guidance primitives; daily mood and opportunity | "Low resistance lets two functions cooperate, making an existing capacity easier to use." |
| Technical | Daily and weekly summaries | "within 0.37°," "7 of 7 sampled days" |

The intimate register is the one the product brief describes, and it is the minority of what users read.

**Benchmark examples.** These should anchor the canonical voice:

- Sky Now Moon–Pluto friction: "A strong reaction may carry more history than the present situation alone can explain." Reflection: "What might this reaction be trying to protect?"
- Sky Now Moon–Neptune friction: "It may be difficult to tell an immediate feeling from an assumption about what someone else feels."
- Sky Now Venus–Neptune friction: "An ideal image may make it difficult to see what is actually being offered or requested."
- Moon in Sagittarius: "…staying present with discomfort long enough to truly process it, instead of only rising above it with optimism."
- Sun in Libra: "When you honor your own center, your diplomacy becomes a strength rather than a way to disappear."
- Natal-target Saturn: "Fear of falling short may turn preparation into delay or standards into punishment."

**Retire:** "Growth comes through…, so… rather than…"; "this is a natural placement"; "you're here to"; "wired"; "two functions"; "This brings [list] into contact with [list]"; "sampled days."

## What the Canonical Naksha Interpretation Standard Should Be

This describes what a finished entry must do. It is a specification, not prose to paste.

**1. The core test.** A reader who knows no astrology can say what this configuration might feel like and look like in their week. A reader shown the text without its label can still tell which configuration it describes.

**2. Required elements of a natal interpretation.**

- **What:** the functions involved, named once in plain language: what the planet does in a person, the sign's style, the house's arena.
- **How:** what this specific combination does to those functions. For aspects, whether the two functions argue, merge, alternate, cooperate, or open a door for each other.
- **Experience:** what it can feel like from the inside, in one or two concrete sentences.
- **Expression:** at least one observable behavior, choice, habit, or relational pattern.
- **Constructive:** how it works well, specifically.
- **Difficult:** what happens when it is overused, unconscious, or rigid, stated without moralizing.
- **Reflection:** one question that only makes sense for this configuration.
- **Context:** how personal the placement is (see 4).

Not every short form needs all eight. Every long form should have them, and their order should vary between entries.

**3. Aspect differentiation.** One pair core per planet pair defines the two functions and their central tension or alliance. Each aspect then inflects it in its own way:

- **Conjunction:** fusion and identification; the two functions are hard to tell apart; strength and blind spot arrive together. Not automatically soft.
- **Square:** friction felt inside the person; overcorrection; pressure to act or adjust.
- **Opposition:** alternation and projection; the tension often arrives through other people; awareness grows through relationship.
- **Trine:** ease, natural talent, the default response; the risk is complacency or an unexamined habit.
- **Sextile:** an available skill or opening that needs initiative.

A pair's conjunction, trine, and sextile must not share a meaning, and neither may its square and opposition.

**4. Tiers of personalness.**

- **Personal:** Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, the rising sign, and every planet's house. Second person, specific.
- **Social:** Jupiter and Saturn signs. Second person, with a light cohort note for Saturn.
- **Generational:** Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto signs, and aspects between outer planets. Framed as shared by a generation, with personal meaning delegated to house and to contacts with personal planets.
- **Rising-sign-determined:** house signs. Framed as the style of an arena, with the ruling planet carrying the personal story.
- **Shared sky:** Sky Now. Collective symbolism first, then what a person might notice if it resonates.
- **Someone else's chart:** neutral or third-person phrasing for guest charts.

**5. Epistemic grammar.**

- Describe tendencies and possibilities. Never state motives as facts or assign identity ("you are wired," "you're here to").
- No childhood, trauma, or family claims unless the configuration is strongly associated with them, and then only as one possibility among several.
- No predictions of outcomes or events: money, partners, longevity.
- Calibrate "may." Use it where uncertainty is real and plain description where the symbolism is clear. One or two hedges per paragraph at most.
- Describe difficulty; do not prescribe a correction. No mandatory self-improvement ending.

**6. Language.**

- At least one concrete, lived detail in every long entry.
- Abstract nouns (transformation, depth, energy, authenticity, growth) only after the concrete thing they summarize.
- At most one adjective triad per entry.
- Plain words for technical terms, each explained once: natal, transit, house, aspect, orb.
- No computational detail in reading prose; orbs and sampling belong in a details view.

**7. System conventions.**

- No MC or IC labels on whole-sign houses. If the MC is shown, compute it and place it.
- State rulership conventions as conventions, consistently, or leave them out.
- Read empty houses through their ruling planet, or describe them as a style rather than a verdict.

**8. Composition.**

- A composed reading is one interpretation with one lead theme, not a stack of fields.
- Hierarchy is explicit: what is strongest, what supports or complicates it, which area of life, what to notice, what to try.
- Practices and prompts are chosen by the mechanism of the lead configuration and say why in one clause.
- Daily guidance is stable for the local day. Weekly guidance has an arc and names time events: stations, ingresses, lunations.

**9. Voice.** The voice of the benchmark entries: observant, warm, plain, and specific, poetic only when an image clarifies. Not therapeutic, clinical, or textbook. Varied sentence structure across entries is a requirement.

**10. Verification.** Each rewritten family gets an editorial lint (banned phrases, ceilings on template frequency, required elements present) and a human check that aspect variants of a pair differ in meaning.

## Recommended Rewrite Order

1. **P0 framing fixes before any rewriting.** Remove the MC and IC labels. Replace the planet summary glue with a neutral presentation of the two texts. Add generational framing to the Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto signs and strip "wired" and "here to." Decide what "today" means for daily guidance.
2. **Write the canonical standard and approve benchmarks.** Choose six to ten benchmark entries, starting from the strongest list above, and agree a banned-phrase list and template ceilings.
3. **Natal aspect model and pair cores.** Write the 45 pair cores first, seeded from Sky Now. Then write aspect inflections for the personal pairs among Sun through Saturn (21 pairs, 105 readings), then the rest, with generational framing for pairs of outer planets.
4. **Sky Now aspect layer.** Reuse the pair cores with sky framing, differentiate all five aspects, add the collective layer for slow pairs, and name the lunar phases.
5. **Daily composition.** Weighted significance, a stable day theme, one synthesized reading, and prompts and practices keyed to the lead configuration.
6. **Weekly composition.** The C3 arc structure, awareness of stations and ingresses, de-duplicated days, and long transits framed as a backdrop.
7. **Planet-in-house.** Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, and Mars first; then Jupiter and Saturn; then the outer planets as the personal expression of generational placements.
8. **House-sign.** Choose between rising-sign profiles with house rulers and a lighter style layer, then write to that model.
9. **Planet-in-sign.** Venus, Jupiter, and Saturn first; Mercury and Mars next; Sun and Moon polish last, since they are closest to standard.
10. **Practices and prompts.** Rebuild practices around mechanisms; re-key prompts.
11. **Voice and presentation.** Rendering (sentence blocks, line breaks), labels, boilerplate, and a final label-removal review across the corpus.

## Disagreements / Ambiguities an Engineering Reviewer Should Resolve

1. **Is guidance prose or spine?** The forecast guidance plan designed these primitives as a "factual spine" for later AI synthesis, with AI adding "warmth/length/dialogue." AI is out of V1, so users read the spine as finished prose. Decide whether V1 ships authored synthesis or presents primitives differently.
2. **What is "today"?** Today's Energy is recomputed from the current instant and its prompt changes with it. One option is a stable local-day reading, for example the most significant transit across the day or the local-noon instant the weekly already uses, with the Moon as a separate "right now" layer. This touches the R6 freshness work.
3. **Significance model.** Tightest orb versus a weighted model (planet importance, applying or separating, stations, duration). Daily excludes Saturn and Jupiter while weekly includes them, and no surface includes Uranus, Neptune, or Pluto transits.
4. **Natal aspect content model.** Hand-author 225 readings, or author 45 pair cores plus five inflections each with editorial review. Either way, `ASPECT_MEANINGS` should stop serving as interpretation.
5. **Shared pair cores.** Sharing cores between natal charts and Sky Now helps consistency, but natal "you are" language could leak into the shared sky. A two-register model, a core plus a context frame, avoids that.
6. **Rulership system.** Modern rulers (Pluto with Scorpio and the 8th) or traditional ones, and whether to adopt Whole Sign house-ruler interpretation for empty houses. This decides the house-sign redesign.
7. **Generational placements in the UI.** Keep them in the personal planet list with cohort framing, move them to a "your generation" section, or de-emphasize them in summaries.
8. **Retrograde and station data.** Adding them changes calculation and should get R5-level verification. The content cannot name a station without it.
9. **Rendering.** `InterpretationCard` renders each sentence as its own block to fix clipping, and the handoff says not to modify it casually. That choice now shapes editorial flow and template visibility. The hard-wrapped newlines in the source should be normalized either way.
10. **Tests that measure meaning.** The Sky Now test checks that five readings per pair are distinct as JSON, which the generic dynamic sentence satisfies. Add editorial tests: banned phrases, template-frequency ceilings, required fields, and a check that a pair's aspect variants differ in their meaning field.
11. **Moon transits.** Should the Moon ever be the daily headline, or always a background layer?
12. **Exposed computation.** Should orbs and "sampled days" appear in reading prose, or only in details?
13. **Dead content.** Delete or surface `SIGN_MEANINGS`, the house-sign fallback flavors, and the aspect long texts.
14. **Guest and children's charts.** Choose a pronoun model for someone else's chart, and decide whether children's charts need different content or a notice.
15. **Rising sign.** The 1st house is the rising sign, but the app names the Ascendant only in a parenthesis in the generic house text, never says "rising sign," and never shows the Ascendant degree. Decide whether to present it explicitly.
